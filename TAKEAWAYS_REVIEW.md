# Takeaways for review

Note on method: For journal articles, abstracts were retrieved either directly from the publisher page, or — when the publisher page redirected to a login wall (common for Elsevier/Springer/Nature/ACS/SAGE) — from bibliographic-metadata APIs (Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, Crossref) that host the publisher-supplied abstract text verbatim. Those are marked below. For the grey-literature reports (items 16–21), the DOI landing pages (eScholarship, IADB) blocked automated fetches (CAPTCHA/403/405), so takeaways for those items are based on secondary descriptions found via web search of official/institutional pages describing the report, not the report's own abstract text — flagged explicitly in each case. Please treat items so flagged with extra scrutiny.

## 2. Life cycle performance and carbon handprint of lithium-ion batteries in electric vehicles
- Abstract source: Semantic Scholar API record for https://doi.org/10.1007/s44498-026-00112-1 (publisher abstract text, hybrid OA, CC BY)
- Draft takeaway: EVs cut US light-duty fleet's energy use, material extraction, and CO2 emissions versus an all-gasoline scenario, despite higher metal mining needs.

## 3. Electrifying light vehicles in the United States shows emission reduction potential for all vehicle types and powertrains
- Abstract source: Semantic Scholar API record for https://doi.org/10.1038/s44458-025-00032-4 (publisher abstract text, gold OA, CC BY)
- Draft takeaway: Electrifying all 2023 US light vehicles sold, especially trucks, would cut lifecycle emissions 59%, with battery electric vehicles beating gasoline and hybrid options.

## 4. The electric vehicle transition: effects on copper supply dynamics in a net-zero future
- Abstract source: OpenAlex API record (reconstructed from abstract_inverted_index) for https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2026.108798
- Draft takeaway: Meeting future EV copper demand needs major new mining, but higher recycling and smaller batteries could cut required new mines from 373 to 87.

## 6. Who Will Have Enough? Battery Mineral Demand and Sufficiency in Vehicle Producing Countries
- Abstract source: Semantic Scholar API record for https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.5c12420 (publisher abstract text)
- Draft takeaway: Minerals for EV batteries are globally sufficient, but countries that manufacture EVs face shortfalls and import dependence, even with strong recycling.

## 7. Paths of progress: Forecasting global electric vehicle demand amidst demographic and economic growth
- Abstract source: OpenAlex API record (reconstructed from abstract_inverted_index) for https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trd.2025.104928
- Draft takeaway: Global light-duty vehicle sales will grow to 93-124 million by 2035, with EV demand reaching up to 73 million if targets are met.

## 8. Policy mechanisms to decarbonize cement production: through the lens of California
- Abstract source: https://doi.org/10.1088/2634-4505/adbc1a (IOPscience article page, direct quotes)
- Draft takeaway: Cement makes large CO2 emissions; policy actions like public procurement and performance-based codes can support California's net-zero cement goals and inform other regions.

## 9. Shifting Manufacturing: Electric Vehicle Supply Strategy using the Model for International EV Trade
- Abstract source: OpenAlex API record (reconstructed from abstract_inverted_index) for https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.144357
- Draft takeaway: Mexico supplies 15% of US vehicles but only 6% of EVs; it must shift half its output to EVs by 2035 to keep market share.

## 10. Short-term exposure to fine particulate pollution and elderly mortality in Chile
- Abstract source: Semantic Scholar API record for https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-024-01634-x (publisher abstract text; also see license check below)
- Draft takeaway: A 10 µg/m³ rise in monthly PM2.5 raises all-cause mortality 1.7% in Chileans over 75, with stronger effects in central-south and metro areas.

## 11. Future of Global Electric Vehicle Supply Chain: Exploring the Impact of Global Trade on Electric Vehicle Production and Battery Requirements
- Abstract source: Semantic Scholar API record for https://doi.org/10.1177/03611981241244797 (publisher abstract text, hybrid OA, CC BY)
- Draft takeaway: A new trade model shows EV production will cluster in regional blocks; rising demand in one country doesn't proportionally boost its own production.

## 12. Life cycle assessment, quo vadis? Supporting or deterring greenwashing? A survey of practitioners
- Abstract source: https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/va/d3va00317e (open-access RSC article; abstract text found via web search of the official RSC page, corroborated by matching partial text from Crossref metadata)
- Draft takeaway: Life cycle assessment results vary widely across practitioners, risking greenwashing; more harmonization is needed to make results consistent and meaningful.

## 13. A systematic review of life cycle greenhouse gas intensity values for hydrogen production pathways
- Abstract source: ScienceDirect page for https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2023.113588, accessed via text-extraction proxy (r.jina.ai); Semantic Scholar/OpenAlex/Crossref all show no abstract for this closed-access record
- Draft takeaway: A review of 85 studies finds hydrogen production's carbon intensity varies widely; renewable-powered electrolysis is lowest, while steam methane reforming is highest.

## 14. Chronic exposure to fine particles (PM2.5) and mortality: Evidence from Chile
- Abstract source: Semantic Scholar API record for http://dx.doi.org/10.1097/EE9.0000000000000253 (publisher abstract text, full Background/Methods/Results/Conclusion abstract)
- Draft takeaway: Three-year average PM2.5 exposure is linked to higher cardiopulmonary and pulmonary mortality across Chilean municipalities, matching findings from wealthier countries.

## 15. Literature review on policies to mitigate GHG emissions for cement and concrete
- Abstract source: OpenAlex API record (reconstructed from abstract_inverted_index) for https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2022.106278
- Draft takeaway: A review of 37 studies finds broad agreement on technical fixes for cement decarbonization, but no consensus yet on which policies work best.
